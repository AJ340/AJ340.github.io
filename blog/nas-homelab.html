Building a NAS I actually trust

I put off building a NAS for years, mostly because I could never justify it as anything other than "a hard drive with extra steps." What changed my mind wasn't storage — it was realizing I'd quietly become dependent on a handful of cloud services I didn't fully trust, for photos, files, and DNS, and I wanted to own that stack instead of renting it.

Why Unraid

I ended up on Unraid rather than TrueNAS or a plain Linux box, mostly for the flexibility: mixed drive sizes in a single array, and a plugin/Docker ecosystem that makes adding a new service a five-minute decision instead of a weekend project. The tradeoff is it's less "pure" than ZFS-first systems, but for a homelab that's evolving constantly, I'd rather optimize for how fast I can try something than for theoretical purity.

The current stack is intentionally boring:

  • Immich for photos — the thing that finally got me off a paid cloud photo backup
  • Nextcloud for general file sync across devices
  • Pi-hole for network-wide DNS filtering, running on the same box

The AI side of the rack

Alongside the NAS I run a separate local AI box — enough GPU headroom to run Ollama and Open WebUI comfortably for day-to-day experimentation, without sending every prompt to a third party. It's not a replacement for hosted models on anything serious, but it's the right place to poke at ideas before they're ready for a real API bill.

What I'd tell past me

Start smaller than you think. I over-planned the drive layout and under-planned backups — the array itself isn't a backup, and treating it like one is the classic mistake. The unglamorous part (an actual off-box backup routine) is the part that matters most, and the part I built last instead of first.

If you're on the fence about a homelab: the return isn't really the money you save versus cloud subscriptions, it's the shape of the problem. Debugging your own infrastructure, on hardware you can see, is a different kind of practice than debugging someone else's — and it's made me better at the day job too.
