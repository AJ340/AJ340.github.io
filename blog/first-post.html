Why this site is built like a status board

Most portfolio sites are brochures: a headshot, some adjectives, a list of logos. I wanted mine to read more like the thing I actually spend my day doing — watching a system, understanding its state, and trusting that what it reports is true.

That's the idea behind the layout here. The status bar at the top isn't decoration — it's the same instinct that makes me check a dashboard before I check my email. Sections are numbered modules instead of a scroll of vague headings, because the content really is a sequence: who I am, what I've built, what I'm writing, how to reach me.

What "hosted here" actually means

The NIGHTFALL demo on the projects page isn't a screenshot or a video — it's the real game, running client-side, no install and no backend. That was a deliberate constraint: anything I claim is "live" on this site has to actually run in your browser right now.

Signal and Keel aren't embedded the same way, and that's also deliberate — Signal needs an API key behind it, and Keel is a private health app by design. Case studies and source links are the honest way to represent those here.

What's next

More posts will land here as I ship — mostly short write-ups on things I got stuck on, decisions I made in the game or the search tools, and whatever else is worth writing down.
